# DESIGN.md

# HIMJA BEHL — Design System & Art Direction

---

## 01 — DESIGN VISION

HIMJA BEHL should feel like a luxury fashion editorial translated into a digital experience.

The website must communicate:
- Luxury
- Taste
- Curation
- Confidence
- Personal attention
- Fashion expertise
- Sophistication
- Modern Indian wedding culture

The experience should feel closer to opening a beautifully designed fashion magazine than visiting a conventional wedding-services website.

The design must feel:
> Editorial, not corporate.
> Fashion-led, not wedding-template-led.
> Personal, not transactional.
> Premium, not decorative.
> Confident, not loud.

The website should never feel like an AI product, SaaS platform, generic portfolio template, or wedding planner website.

---

## 02 — DESIGN REFERENCE

The primary conceptual reference is the visual language of premium fashion/editorial personal-brand websites, particularly the feeling of:
- Large typography
- Strong photography
- Magazine-style composition
- Generous whitespace
- Editorial storytelling
- Asymmetric layouts
- Intentional typography
- Minimal interface chrome
- Personal brand presence

A useful reference point is the editorial/personal-brand quality of:
`https://kamyagupta.com/`

The goal is NOT to copy the reference. The goal is to extract its principles:
- Personal identity
- Editorial storytelling
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Large type
- Photography as content
- Sections that feel like magazine spreads
- Controlled asymmetry
- Premium simplicity

HIMJA BEHL must have its own visual identity.

---

## 03 — CORE ART DIRECTION

The visual system should combine:

### Luxury Fashion Editorial
Strong typography, photography, negative space, asymmetry and carefully controlled layouts.

### Modern Indian Wedding
Subtle references to Indian wedding culture through:
- Rich colour
- Textiles
- Silhouettes
- Jewellery
- Architecture
- Wedding photography
- Cultural detail

These references should appear through actual content and imagery rather than decorative clichés.

### Personal Brand
Himja herself must remain visible.
The website should feel like entering her world, not browsing a faceless agency.

---

## 04 — DESIGN RATIO

The overall visual balance should approximately follow:
- **70% — Luxury Fashion Editorial**
- **20% — Personal Brand**
- **10% — Interactive Technology**

Technology should support the visual experience. It should never become the visual identity.

---

## 05 — DESIGN PERSONALITY

Every design decision should feel:
- Elegant
- Intelligent
- Curated
- Mature
- Contemporary
- Slightly unexpected
- Quietly luxurious

Avoid designs that feel:
- Cute
- Cartoonish
- Generic
- Overly feminine
- Overly traditional
- Corporate
- Tech-heavy
- Excessively glamorous
- Visually noisy

---

## 06 — COLOUR SYSTEM

The colour palette should be restrained. The hero establishes the strongest colour identity.

### Primary Deep Red
Use a deep maroon / wine red as the principal brand colour.
Suggested starting value: `#641D26`
This is a starting point, not a rigid requirement.
The final shade should be adjusted against the actual photography and typography.

The red should feel:
- Deep
- Rich
- Mature
- Fashion-oriented
- Luxurious

Avoid bright red. Avoid generic burgundy gradients.

### Warm White
Primary light background: `#F6F3EE`
Use warm white instead of pure white for major editorial sections.
This creates a softer luxury-fashion feel.

### Soft Ivory
Secondary light surface: `#EEE9E1`
Use sparingly for visual separation.

### Deep Charcoal
Primary dark text: `#171515`
Avoid using absolute black for normal typography unless specifically required.

### Pure Black
`#000000`
Reserved for:
- Logo variations
- Strong graphic moments
- Certain editorial sections
- High-contrast design treatments

### Colour Usage
The site should primarily operate within:
- Deep Maroon
- Warm White
- Soft Ivory
- Deep Charcoal
- Black

Do not introduce many additional colours. Colour should come from the brand palette, photography, and wedding imagery, not from decorative UI elements.

---

## 07 — TYPOGRAPHY

Typography is one of the most important elements of the design. The typography should create the feeling of a premium fashion publication.

### Primary Display Typeface
Use an elegant editorial serif for major headlines.
Characteristics:
- High contrast
- Fashion/editorial character
- Refined
- Sophisticated
- Strong at large sizes

Potential directions include:
- Cormorant Garamond
- Playfair Display
- DM Serif Display
- Instrument Serif
- A similarly refined editorial serif

Do not use a decorative wedding font.

### Secondary Typeface
Use a clean modern sans-serif for:
- Navigation
- Body text
- Labels
- Buttons
- Metadata
- Forms
- Small UI elements

Potential directions:
- Inter
- Manrope
- DM Sans
- Geist
- A similarly clean contemporary sans-serif

---

## 08 — TYPOGRAPHIC HIERARCHY

Typography should create dramatic hierarchy.

### Hero Headline
Large and dominant.
- Desktop: Approximately `clamp(4rem, 8vw, 8rem)`
- Mobile: Approximately `clamp(3rem, 14vw, 5rem)`

### Section Headlines
Large editorial serif.
Approximately: `clamp(3rem, 6vw, 6rem)`

### Subheadings
Modern sans or serif depending on context.

### Body
Comfortable reading size: Approximately `16px–20px`.
Line-height should generally be generous.

### Microcopy
Small uppercase sans-serif. Used for:
- Categories
- Numbers
- Navigation
- Metadata
- Labels
Example: `01 — DISCOVER`
Use increased letter spacing.

---

## 09 — TYPOGRAPHIC RULES

Typography should not fill every available space.
Use:
- Large type
- Short paragraphs
- Strong line breaks
- Generous whitespace

Avoid:
- Long walls of text
- Excessive bolding
- Too many font sizes
- Excessive uppercase text
- Decorative typography everywhere

Large typography should feel intentional.

---

## 10 — LOGO

The primary wordmark: **HIMJA BEHL**
The logo should be minimal and premium.
Preferred treatment:
- Black on light backgrounds
- White on dark backgrounds
- Strong spacing
- No decorative icon unless separately approved

The design should communicate connection, balance, curation, structure, and distinctiveness. The logo must remain extremely simple.

Do not add:
- Crowns
- Wedding rings
- Floral motifs
- Fashion silhouettes
- Generic monograms
- Excessive ornamental lines

---

## 11 — GRID SYSTEM

The site should use an editorial grid rather than a conventional centered-card layout.
Desktop should generally operate on a **12-column grid** with generous outer margins.

Important layouts may use:
- 12-column layouts
- 8-column content
- 4-column supporting content
- Full-bleed photography
- Overlapping elements

The grid should provide structure while allowing controlled asymmetry.

---

## 12 — CONTAINER

Desktop content should generally maintain generous margins.
Approximate starting point:
- Desktop: `48px–80px` horizontal padding
- Tablet: `32px–48px`
- Mobile: `20px–24px`

These values are flexible. Visual balance takes priority over rigid numbers.

---

## 13 — WHITESPACE

Whitespace is a major part of the luxury aesthetic.
Sections should breathe. Avoid compressing content simply to reduce page length.
Large sections should often contain significant negative space.
Whitespace should communicate confidence.

---

## 14 — HERO DESIGN

The hero is the strongest visual statement of the entire website.

### Background
Deep maroon / wine red.

### Main Composition
Use a high-quality transparent PNG or cutout composition featuring well-dressed wedding individuals.
The composition may overlap typography, hero boundaries, or grid, but must remain controlled.

### Hero Typography
The headline should be extremely prominent.
It should feel like a fashion campaign headline.

### Hero CTA
Primary CTA should be visually obvious but minimal.
Suggested: `START YOUR STYLING JOURNEY`
Secondary: `EXPLORE THE JOURNEYS`

Avoid large SaaS-style button groups.

---

## 15 — HERO NAVIGATION

The navigation initially sits naturally within the hero.
The navbar should visually integrate with the maroon background.
As the user scrolls:
- Navbar becomes sticky
- Background treatment may transition
- Text/logo colour may adapt
- Navigation remains unobtrusive

The transition should feel like part of the page rather than a UI component suddenly appearing.

---

## 16 — NAVIGATION STYLE

Navigation typography:
- Small
- Uppercase or restrained title case
- Sans-serif
- Slight letter spacing

Avoid oversized navigation. The logo should remain the strongest element in the navigation.

---

## 17 — STORY SECTION

The story section introduces Himja personally.
The composition should feel like an editorial profile spread.

Possible layout:
```text
[ IMAGE ]

                 ABOUT HIMJA
                 Large headline

                 Editorial paragraph
                 Supporting details
```

Do not simply place an image next to a generic text card.

---

## 18 — STORY IMAGE TREATMENT

Photography should feel natural.
Avoid:
- Heavy filters
- Excessive gradients
- Fake film effects
- Generic masks
- Excessive rounded corners

Preferred treatments:
- Full-bleed images
- Editorial crops
- Tall portrait crops
- Asymmetric image placement
- Controlled overlap

Images should generally retain strong rectangular editorial proportions.

---

## 19 — MARQUEE / SCROLLER

The horizontal scroller should act as a visual transition.
It can contain short phrases such as:
- `CURATED NOT COMPROMISED`
- `EVERY LOOK HAS A STORY`
- `THE ART OF THE WEDDING WARDROBE`

The typography should be large enough to feel like part of the editorial composition.
Motion should be slow and subtle.

---

## 20 — JOURNEYS SECTION

The client journeys section should feel like a magazine archive.
The section should not resemble: Google reviews, testimonial cards, star ratings, SaaS testimonial sliders, or standard portfolio cards.
Instead, use large editorial imagery and storytelling.

---

## 21 — JOURNEY CARD DESIGN

A journey card should contain:
- Strong image
- Client name
- Small category/context
- Short supporting text
- Read-more interaction

Cards may intentionally vary in size.
This creates an editorial rhythm.

---

## 22 — JOURNEY HOVER

Desktop hover may reveal:
- Journey title
- Short description
- Arrow
- Subtle image scale
- Colour transition

Keep interactions subtle. Do not use excessive zoom.

---

## 23 — JOURNEY PAGE

Individual journeys should feel like editorial magazine articles.
The page should prioritize:
- Photography
- Typography
- Story
- Styling details

The page should not feel like a case-study dashboard.

---

## 24 — JOURNEY PAGE LAYOUT

Suggested rhythm:
```text
FULL-BLEED HERO IMAGE

CLIENT NAME
WEDDING / CONTEXT

INTRODUCTION

THE BRIEF

LARGE IMAGE

THE PROCESS

IMAGE + TEXT

THE EDIT

IMAGE GRID

THE RESULT

CLIENT WORDS

VISUAL ARCHIVE

NEXT JOURNEY
```

Use varied section compositions rather than repeating the same two-column layout.

---

## 25 — PROCESS SECTION

The process section should feel structured but not corporate.
The four stages:
- `01 DISCOVER`
- `02 CURATE`
- `03 REFINE`
- `04 DELIVER`

Possible layout:
- Large numbers
- Thin dividers
- Short descriptions
- Generous spacing

Avoid icon-heavy process cards.

---

## 26 — PROCESS VISUAL LANGUAGE

Use typography and spacing rather than illustrations.
Thin rules/dividers can be used extensively. Dividers should be subtle.

---

## 27 — ENQUIRY SECTION

The enquiry form is an important part of the visual experience.
It should feel like entering a private styling consultation.
Avoid: large white form cards, SaaS form styling, dense field layouts, excessive borders, generic dropdown-heavy forms.

---

## 28 — ENQUIRY EXPERIENCE

Questions should appear progressively.
Each question should have:
- Large editorial question
- Minimal controls
- Clear selection state
- Progress indicator
- Next/back interaction

The interface should feel calm and premium.

---

## 29 — FORM QUESTION TYPOGRAPHY

Example:
`WHO ARE WE STYLING?` (Large serif question)
Options: BRIDE, GROOM, COUPLE, FAMILY, MULTIPLE.
Options should feel like editorial selections rather than standard HTML controls.

---

## 30 — FORM SELECTION STATES

Selected options should have a clearly visible state.
Possible treatments:
- Filled background
- Border change
- Text inversion
- Small marker
- Underline

Do not rely solely on colour. The selected state should remain elegant.

---

## 31 — FORM PROGRESS

Use a minimal progress indicator.
Examples: `01 / 06` or `YOUR EDIT — 03`
The progress indicator should be small and unobtrusive.

---

## 32 — THE HIMJA EDIT

The personalized result should feel like a premium editorial page.
It should NOT resemble: an AI chatbot, a personality test, a dashboard, a generated report, or a SaaS result screen.

---

## 33 — THE HIMJA EDIT VISUAL STRUCTURE

Suggested layout:
```text
THE HIMJA EDIT
YOUR STYLE DIRECTION

MODERN ROMANTIC

ELEGANT
CONTEMPORARY
EXPRESSIVE

[EDITORIAL DESCRIPTION]

PALETTE
[COLOUR SWATCHES]

DIRECTION
[SHORT STYLING DIRECTION]

```

Typography should dominate.

---

## 34 — STYLE TAGS

Style tags should be understated.
Examples: ELEGANT, CONTEMPORARY, TEXTURED, TIMELESS.
Use thin borders or typographic separation. Avoid pill-heavy UI.

---

## 35 — COLOUR PALETTE DISPLAY

The palette should be represented visually using simple rectangular or circular swatches.
Do not make it look like a design-tool colour picker.

---

## 36 — CTA DESIGN

Buttons should be minimal.
Preferred visual direction:
- Thin border
- Slightly generous horizontal padding
- Small uppercase sans-serif
- Subtle hover transition

Avoid: Huge pill buttons, gradient buttons, excessive shadows, 3D effects.

---

## 37 — BUTTON BEHAVIOUR

Hover states should be refined.
Possible interaction:
- Default: Transparent / restrained
- Hover: Background fills, text inverts
- Active: Subtle scale or press effect

Animation should be quick and understated.

---

## 38 — BORDERS & DIVIDERS

Use thin borders frequently.
Preferred: 1px low-contrast borders.
Avoid thick UI borders.

---

## 39 — BORDER RADIUS

The design should not depend heavily on rounded cards.
Preferred: Mostly square / slightly rounded.
Avoid `20px–30px` radius everywhere.

---

## 40 — SHADOWS

Use shadows sparingly.
Most of the design should rely on contrast, typography, spacing, photography, and borders, rather than floating cards.

---

## 41 — ICONOGRAPHY

Icons should be minimal.
If icons are necessary: Thin, simple, geometric, consistent stroke width.
Avoid: Wedding icons, decorative line-art illustrations, generic stock icons.

---

## 42 — IMAGE DIRECTION

Photography is a primary design element.
Preferred imagery: Editorial portraits, wedding fashion, full outfits, close-up textile details, jewellery, accessories, movement, architectural environments.

---

## 43 — IMAGE COMPOSITION

Prefer: Vertical portraits, wide cinematic images, full-body fashion images, detail crops, intentional negative space.
Avoid repetitive image grids.

---

## 44 — IMAGE TREATMENT

Images should generally remain natural.
Avoid excessive colour grading, grain, vignette, blur, glow, or artificial overlays.

---

## 45 — IMAGE ASYMMETRY

Not every image should be centered.
Use offset images, cropped edges, overlapping images, full-bleed photography.

---

## 46 — SECTION TRANSITIONS

Sections should not all look like independent blocks.
Possible transitions: Colour changes, full-width imagery, typography-only moments, marquee, overlapping photography, large whitespace.

---

## 47 — DARK / LIGHT RHYTHM

The website should alternate between visual moods.
Example:
```text
MAROON HERO
↓
WARM WHITE STORY
↓
EDITORIAL MARQUEE
↓
LIGHT JOURNEYS
↓
DARK / CONTRAST PROCESS
↓
LIGHT OR MAROON ENQUIRY
↓
DARK FOOTER
```

---

## 48 — MOTION DIRECTION

Motion should feel slow, elegant, intentional, smooth, and cinematic.
Use motion to reveal content. Do not use motion simply because an animation is technically possible.

---

## 49 — SCROLL ANIMATIONS

Good examples: Text revealing upward, images gently entering viewport, masked image reveals, subtle parallax.
Avoid: Excessive bouncing, fast zooms, spinning objects, large elastic effects.

---

## 50 — MICRO-INTERACTIONS

Interactions should reward exploration.
Examples: Button hover, image hover, arrow movement, form option selection.
They should remain secondary to the content.

---

## 51 — CURSOR INTERACTIONS

Custom cursor behaviour may be considered for desktop, but must remain subtle.
Mobile must not depend on cursor interactions.

---

## 52 — RESPONSIVE DESIGN

Responsive design should be intentional.
- **Desktop**: Large typography, asymmetric compositions, multi-column layouts, full-bleed photography.
- **Tablet**: Reduce typography scale, grid complexity, image overlap.
- **Mobile**: Prioritize typography, photography, clear hierarchy, simple navigation. Avoid trying to preserve every desktop composition exactly.

---

## 53 — MOBILE HERO

The hero PNG composition must be carefully adapted for mobile.
The composition should not crop important faces, make the headline unreadable, or cause excessive page height.

---

## 54 — MOBILE NAVIGATION

Use a simple navigation pattern.
The menu should open into a full-screen or large overlay.
Avoid a generic mobile drawer if a more editorial solution can be achieved.

---

## 55 — MOBILE FORMS

Forms should become especially simple on mobile.
Use: Large tap targets, one question at a time, minimal typing, fixed progression controls.

---

## 56 — ACCESSIBILITY IN DESIGN

Accessibility must coexist with the luxury aesthetic.
Never sacrifice usability for visual minimalism.
Ensure text is readable, focus is visible, and motion can be reduced.

---

## 57 — DESIGN TOKENS

Use centralized design variables for:
- Colours (`--color-maroon`, `--color-warm-white`, `--color-ivory`, `--color-charcoal`, `--color-black`)
- Typography (`--font-display`, `--font-sans`)
- Spacing (e.g. 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 128, 160)

---

## 58 — DESIGN CONSISTENCY

Consistent across all pages: Typography, button language, spacing, image treatment, border treatment, navigation, motion, colour system, editorial tone.

---

## 59 — WHAT TO AVOID

Explicitly discouraged:
- **Generic Wedding Aesthetic**: No ring icons, floral borders, excessive gold.
- **Generic Luxury Aesthetic**: No black+gold everywhere, fake luxury gradients, metallic effects.
- **Generic AI Aesthetic**: No purple/blue AI gradients, glowing blobs, "AI POWERED" badges.
- **Generic SaaS Aesthetic**: No excessive cards, huge rounded containers, dashboard layouts, pricing-table visuals.

---

## 60 — DESIGN QUALITY BAR

Every section should pass the following test:
- Does it feel intentional?
- Does it feel premium?
- Does it feel like Himja?
- Does photography matter?
- Does the typography have hierarchy?
- Does the page breathe?
- Does the interaction add value?

---

## 61 — CONTENT + DESIGN RELATIONSHIP

Design should adapt to the actual content.
Do not force content into predetermined templates if doing so damages the editorial composition.

---

## 62 — EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE

The website should behave like a magazine.
It uses scale, typography, photography, white space, rhythm, cropping, and composition.

---

## 63 — PERSONAL BRAND PRINCIPLE

Himja should be visible enough that visitors remember the person behind the service.
The website should communicate: "This is Himja's eye."

---

## 64 — CONVERSION PRINCIPLE

Luxury does not mean hiding the CTA.
CTAs should feel like invitations rather than advertisements.
Preferred language: `START YOUR STYLING JOURNEY`, `EXPLORE THE JOURNEYS`
Avoid aggressive language: `BUY NOW`, `BOOK NOW!!!`

---

## 65 — FINAL DESIGN STATEMENT

The finished website should feel like a luxury fashion editorial dedicated to the art of the wedding wardrobe, with Himja Behl at its center.
It should be visually memorable without being flashy, premium without relying on clichés, and interactive without feeling like a tech demo.

**The design should make a visitor want Himja's eye, not merely Himja's service.**
